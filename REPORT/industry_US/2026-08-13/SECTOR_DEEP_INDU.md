## 5 · Frame transfer (the one question, answered): **does take-or-pay / RPO lock-in / regulated-return apply to defense backlog?**

**Answer: PARTIALLY, and the filing draws the line itself — funded backlog behaves like a take-or-pay
obligation; unfunded backlog does not, and it is 23.7% of GD's total.**

Quoted from the GD 10-Q (2026-07-05), verbatim: the funded portion *"includes items that have been
**authorized and appropriated by the U.S. Congress and funded by customers**, as well as commitments
by international customers that are approved and funded similarly by their governments."* And the
unfunded portion *"includes the amounts we believe are likely to be funded, but **there is no
guarantee that future budgets and appropriations will provide the same funding level** currently
anticipated for a given program."*

| Frame | Transfers? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Take-or-pay (ENRG/midstream)** | **Funded: yes-analogue.** Unfunded: **no.** | Funded = money already appropriated and obligated. Unfunded = an *expectation* the filing explicitly refuses to guarantee. |
| **RPO lock-in (software)** | **Yes on form, no on the escape hatch.** | Total backlog **is** RPO by GD's own equation. But defense contracts carry **termination-for-convenience**, which software RPO does not — the customer can cancel unilaterally. ⚠ **[unverified in this run]** — I read the backlog note, **not** the termination clause; it is named as an unread constraint, not asserted. |
| **Regulated return (UTIL)** | **NO — and this is the important negative.** | There is no rate base and no allowed ROE. Margin is set by **contract type and programme maturity**, which the filing states: *"New long-term production contracts typically have lower margins initially, and then margins generally increase as we achieve learning-curve improvements."* |

⇒ ★ **The correct frame for defense backlog is neither take-or-pay nor regulated return: it is a
learning-curve contract book whose *funded* portion is appropriated cash and whose *unfunded* portion
is a political forecast.** Any "margin must mean-revert" claim on these names therefore has a **named
contract mechanism** — **learning-curve maturation on long-cycle production, disclosed in the filing**
— and the direction of that mechanism at GD right now is **upward, not reverting**: consolidated
operating margin **10.4% in Q2-26 vs 10.0% a year prior**, and **10.4% vs 10.2% for the six months**,
which the company attributes to *"improved operating performance in our Aerospace and Marine Systems
segments."* **The contracted share is NOT marked `unknown` (C3): it is 76.3%, read from the filing.**
